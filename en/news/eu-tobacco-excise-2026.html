European Parliament Rejects Tobacco Tax Directive Recast, Leaving Cigar Tax Hike in Limbo

On June 17, 2026 the European Parliament rejected the proposed recast of the Tobacco Taxation Directive, which would have sharply raised minimum excise rates on cigars and cigarillos. The file now rests with compromise talks in the Council.

Published
November 9, 2026
Announced
June 17, 2026
Maker
Industry statistics (Cigar Association of America data)

The European Parliament has rejected the proposed recast of the EU's Tobacco Taxation Directive. On June 17, 2026, Parliament voted down the recast, while on the same day it approved, by 325 votes to 304 with 25 abstentions, a related proposal to amend Directive (EU) 2020/262 on the general arrangements for excise duty, aligning the movement and control rules with the wider scope the revision would have created.

The recast, presented by the European Commission in July 2025, would have substantially raised minimum excise rates across tobacco categories — including cigars and cigarillos — as the Japanese edition's review of the Commission proposal records. The wider package would also have extended the scope of excise control to new tobacco and nicotine products and to raw tobacco. With Parliament's rejection, the sharp rate increase for cigars is left in limbo.

The file is not closed, however: tax measures of this kind require unanimity in the Council, and discussions on compromise texts for the two directives have resumed under the Irish Presidency. A compromise put to Coreper in June 2026 failed to secure sufficient support, and the working party is now aiming for a general approach at the ECOFIN meeting of November 9, 2026.
