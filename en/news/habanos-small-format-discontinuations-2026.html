Mass Discontinuation of Small-Format Habanos — All Puritos Dropped, Canada Machine-Made Prices Up 10%

A large number of machine-made, small-format Cuban cigars will be discontinued as supplier Promocigar revises its strategy and portfolio for 2027: the José L. Piedra Minis, Montecristo Open Clubs and Minis, Guantanamera Minis, Décimos, Minutos and Cristales (10-count box), and all Puritos. The lines end once packaging stock runs out, in all international markets, for internal strategic reasons rather than sales performance. The direct impact on Japanese shelves is limited, but the lines will disappear from overseas mail order; Canada also raises machine-made prices by 10 percent from February 2027.

Published
November 21, 2026
Announced
November 18, 2026
Maker
Habanos S.A.

According to Halfwheel, Havana House, the Canadian distributor of Habanos, has informed its retailers that a large number of machine-made, small-format Cuban cigars will be discontinued as supplier Promotora de Cigarros, S.L. (Promocigar) revises its strategy and portfolio for 2027. The lines will end once packaging stock runs out, and another distributor has confirmed the changes will affect all international markets, not just Canada. The discontinued products are the José L. Piedra Minis (tins of 20), Montecristo Open Clubs (tins of 20), Montecristo Open Minis (tins of 20), Guantanamera Minis (tins of 20), Guantanamera Décimos (five- and 10-count boxes), Guantanamera Minutos (three-packs and 20-count boxes), Guantanamera Cristales (10-count boxes only), and all Puritos (five-packs and 25-count boxes, from Guantanamera, Montecristo, Partagás and Romeo y Julieta). Havana House says the decision was driven by the supplier's internal strategic and portfolio considerations as part of its planning for 2027, and is not a reflection of any individual product's performance, popularity or demand. Canadian retailers describe the complete loss of the Puritos lines as leaving a large void.

What remains in place: five offerings each in the Clubs and Minis lines from Cohiba, Montecristo, Partagás and Romeo y Julieta, and four Shorts offerings — Cohiba, Montecristo, Trinidad, and a new Romeo y Julieta Short coming in 2027 after being shown in Macau — while Guantanamera Cristales continues in boxes of five and 25. Separately, a source familiar with the changes says all Minis and Clubs in packs of 10 are also planned for delisting. That point is not part of the official notice — Canada stopped carrying the 10-packs in 2020 with the switch to plain packaging, so they do not appear in it — and the same source suggests the supplier is pushing consumers toward the Short and Wide Short formats. The insider account should be read separately from the confirmed list.

One point needs care: whether the Partagás Chicos is included in "all Puritos" has not been confirmed. In Japan's listings, "Partagás Puritos" and "Partagás Chicos" appear as separate items, and they should not be conflated. Halfwheel has also published a correction: Quintero is not part of Promocigar's portfolio, as it is a handmade cigar, and is not being discontinued. José L. Piedra's handmade lines are likewise unaffected, as are non-Cuban products sold under the "Puritos" name.

Halfwheel also reports that prices for all machine-made products sold in Canada will increase by 10 percent as of February 1, 2027, in response to rising supplier costs, and that the increase will also apply to products scheduled for discontinuation while stock lasts. The increase itself concerns Canadian retail pricing, but any spillover to other markets is worth watching.

Cross-checking against this site's Japanese cigar database, ten of the affected small-format items appear on Japan's authorised price list (including the Guantanamera Puritos at ¥440 per cigar, Décimos at ¥660, Romeo y Julieta Puritos at ¥484, Partagás Puritos at ¥1,500, and the Montecristo Open Clubs and Minis). However, the only one with confirmed domestic retail sale in Japan is the Guantanamera Cristales — and only its 10-count box is being discontinued, while the five- and 25-count boxes continue. The direct impact on Japanese shop shelves is therefore limited. The real impact is that these small cigars will disappear from overseas mail order and personal importing. As of November 21, 2026, overseas retailers still list them: CigarTerminal shows the Montecristo Puritos at $59 for 25 and $119 for 50, and the Romeo y Julieta Puritos at $49 (sale price) for 25 and $99 for 50; TopCubans lists the Montecristo Puritos at $53 for 25 and $140 for a 50-count cube, the Partagás Puritos at $90 for a set of 50, and the Partagás Chicos in 25s, alongside the Open Mini, Open Club and José L. Piedra Mini. Once packaging stock is gone, these listings will go with it.

Small-format Habanos discontinuations: José L. Piedra Minis, Montecristo Open Clubs / Minis, Guantanamera Minis / Décimos / Minutos / Cristales (10-count box only), and all Puritos (Guantanamera, Montecristo, Partagás, Romeo y Julieta) / Decided by Promocigar; ends once packaging stock runs out; affects all international markets / Remaining: five Clubs and five Minis offerings, four Shorts offerings (a new Romeo y Julieta Short arrives in 2027), Guantanamera Cristales continues in five- and 25-count boxes / Delisting of 10-packs is an insider account, not officially confirmed / Whether Partagás Chicos is included is unconfirmed / Canada: machine-made prices up 10% from February 1, 2027
