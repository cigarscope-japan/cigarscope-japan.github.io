New U.S. Customs Rule May Affect Cigar Imports by Foreign Importers, Effective January 5, 2027

U.S. Customs and Border Protection has published an interim final rule strengthening the requirements placed on foreign importers. CTPAT certification or the use of a certified customs broker will be mandatory, raising concerns about import restructuring and price effects for cigars. The rule takes effect on January 5, 2027.

Published
November 13, 2026
Announced
November 12, 2026
Maker
Industry statistics (Cigar Association of America data)

U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) published an interim final rule on November 6 strengthening the requirements placed on foreign importers. Foreign importers of record will be required either to be certified under CTPAT (the Customs Trade Partnership Against Terrorism) or to use a certified customs broker. Even U.S. companies in which foreign capital holds a majority stake may fall within the rule's scope.

Cigar manufacturers and importers may be forced to restructure their import operations and absorb additional costs, and there are concerns about supply delays and knock-on effects on retail prices. The rule takes effect on January 5, 2027, and public comments are being accepted until the same day. Note that CRA (Cigar Rights of America) is a cigar advocacy organisation, so its reporting may carry a positional bias; the facts, however, have been verified against the official gazette, the Federal Register (91 FR 69978, November 6, 2026).
