UK Tobacco and Vapes Act Becomes Law, Banning Tobacco Sales to Anyone Born From 2009 On

The Tobacco and Vapes Act received Royal Assent on April 29, 2026, making the UK the first country in Europe with a generational tobacco ban. Anyone born on or after January 1, 2009 will never legally be sold tobacco, cigars included.

Published
November 9, 2026
Announced
April 29, 2026
Maker
Industry statistics (Cigar Association of America data)

The United Kingdom's Tobacco and Vapes Act received Royal Assent on April 29, 2026, and is now law. At its center is a generational ban: anyone born on or after January 1, 2009 will never legally be sold tobacco in the UK. According to Action on Smoking and Health (ASH), the UK is the first country in Europe to pass such a law, and the measure is expected to protect around 4 million young people from tobacco sales within the next five years.

The policy was first introduced to Parliament by former Prime Minister Rishi Sunak, who welcomed the Act, saying it puts the UK "on track to end smoking within a generation." Government modelling cited by ASH projects smoking prevalence among 14-to-30-year-olds falling from 11.2% in 2023 to effectively zero by 2050, and estimates cumulative UK healthcare savings of £6.6 billion.

Because the ban applies to tobacco products generally rather than to cigarettes alone, premium cigars sold in the UK fall within the law's scope for the protected generation. Health groups are now urging ministers to pair the Act with the biggest national quit effort in a decade, so that the millions of adults who still smoke are helped to stop as well.
