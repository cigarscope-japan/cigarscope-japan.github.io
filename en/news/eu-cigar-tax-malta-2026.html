EU Cigar Tax Rise Stalls as Malta Withholds Consent — 26 Countries Back the Compromise, Next Round December 11

At the November 9 meeting of EU finance ministers, 26 member states backed a compromise on raising the EU minimum cigar tax, but Malta withheld its consent and the reform stalled, since tax matters require unanimity. The current minimum is EUR 12 per 1,000 cigars (5 percent); the compromise would raise it gradually to EUR 80 (30 percent) by 2032. The next round of talks is set for December 11.

The European Commission's July 2025 proposal to raise the EU minimum tax rate on cigars stalled at the November 9 meeting of EU finance ministers: 26 member states backed a compromise text, but Malta withheld its consent, and because tax matters require unanimity the reform is stuck. The issue is due to be taken up again on December 11.

The current minimum rate is EUR 12 per 1,000 cigars (5 percent of the retail price). The Commission's original proposal would have raised it to EUR 143 (40 percent); the Council compromise text of November 6 softened that to a gradual rise to EUR 80 (30 percent) by 2032 — holding at EUR 12 (5 percent) from July 2028 through 2029, rising to EUR 50 (20 percent) in 2030–31 and EUR 80 (30 percent) from 2032.

Malta reserved its consent on the grounds that taxation is a national matter, arguing that the proposed minimum rates do not sufficiently take account of differences in national price levels. Sweden already taxes cigars at SEK 1.83 per cigar (2026), above both the Commission and compromise levels, so it would be little affected directly.
