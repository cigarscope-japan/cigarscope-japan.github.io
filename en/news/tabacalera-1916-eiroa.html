Santi Eiroa's 1916 Tabacalera Traces the Family Story in an All-Honduran Blend

The fourth generation of the Eiroa family, Santi Eiroa, builds his first start-to-finish line around five-year-aged habano and the rare Pinareño leaf — five sizes at $16 to $19, sold through only about 100 U.S. retailers.

Published
November 9, 2026
Announced
May 30, 2026
Release
Late May 2026 (shipments began)
Maker
C.L.E. Cigar Co.

The Eiroa family's story starts in 1916, when Generoso Eiroa planted his first tobacco in Pinar del Río, Cuba. After the revolution the family left the island — in 1961, by House of Grauer's account — and went on to help build Honduras's tobacco industry. The new 1916 Tabacalera Eiroa is the work of the fourth generation: Santi Eiroa, youngest son of C.L.E. Cigar Co. founder Christian Eiroa, and the first project he has taken from start to finish, packaging included.

The blend is entirely Honduran, made at the CLE factory. A five-year-aged habano leaf serves as both wrapper and binder, over fillers of corojo and pinareño. Pinareño is the blend's talking point: a variety brought from Cuba to Honduras by Tino Agurdin in 1960, prized for flavour but low-yielding and susceptible to disease. Both CLE and JRE Tobacco have been re-evaluating the leaf in recent years.

Five sizes — Robusto (5 x 50), El Gordo (5 1/2 x 60), Toro (6 x 52), Belicoso (6 1/2 x 50) and The Personal (5 1/2 x 44) — come in boxes of 18 at $16 to $19. The line is regular production but deliberately narrow: CLE says only about 100 retailers in the United States will carry it. It was shown at PCA 2026 in April and began shipping in late May. Santi Eiroa has positioned the blend between the generations — milder than his father Christian's taste, fuller than his grandfather Julio's.
