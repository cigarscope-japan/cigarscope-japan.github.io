Cuba's 2026-27 Tobacco Season Opens under an Energy Crisis

Tabacuba targets 20,000 hectares and 22,000 tons, with half the plantings to be irrigated by renewable power, but its agricultural director warns the season 'will be difficult again'.

Published
November 9, 2026
Announced
October 12, 2026
Maker
Habanos S.A.

Cuba’s 2026–27 tobacco season has officially kicked off in Pinar del Río, with the state-owned Tabacuba group planning to plant 20,000 hectares nationwide — more than 12,000 of them in Pinar del Río province — for an expected collection of 22,000 tons of tobacco, Cigars-connect reports. Pinar del Río, home to Vuelta Abajo, generally accounts for about two-thirds of the tobacco harvest destined for export Havanas.

The season begins under strain. Last season, half of the irrigation systems depended on the electricity grid and growers suffered badly from power cuts, which affected yields, Tabacuba president Marino Murillo recalled; this year, about half of the 20,000 hectares will be irrigated by systems powered by renewable energy, mainly photovoltaic. Even so, Osvaldo Santana Vera, Tabacuba’s agricultural director, quoted by the Spanish news agency EFE, warns that the season “will be difficult again” because of the US oil blockade, which has worsened the crisis since January 2026 — the same social and energy crisis that led to the cancellation of the Festival del Habano scheduled for late February 2026. In 2025–26, 11,426 hectares were planted, against an initial target of 14,623.
