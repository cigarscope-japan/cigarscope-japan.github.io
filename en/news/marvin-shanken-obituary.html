Marvin R. Shanken, Founder of Cigar Aficionado, Dies at 83

The M. Shanken Communications founder, who launched Cigar Aficionado in 1992 and helped turn cigars into luxury products, died on October 29; U.S. premium imports roughly quadrupled within five years of his magazine’s debut.

Published
November 9, 2026
Announced
October 29, 2026
Maker
M. Shanken Communications

Marvin R. Shanken, the founder of M. Shanken Communications Inc. and the editor and publisher of Cigar Aficionado magazine, died on October 29 at the age of 83. Over a five-decade career he dramatically shaped the cigar world as well as the wine, spirits, beer and restaurant industries, helping to transform the once-foundering American cigar business and triggering a newfound appreciation for handmade cigars.

Shanken began in publishing in 1972, buying a small beverage-alcohol newsletter named Impact with $5,000 borrowed from his sister; in 1979 he bought the struggling free newspaper The Wine Spectator and grew it into a glossy magazine read by more than three million wine lovers. In the fall of 1992 he released the premiere issue of Cigar Aficionado, created out of his longtime love of cigars. The magazine showed the world that cigars were luxury products rather than commodities, and in less than five years after its debut, imports of premium handmade cigars into the United States roughly quadrupled, from about 100 million to well over 400 million.

He went on to found The Big Smoke consumer event in New York in 1993 and interviewed many of his magazine's cover subjects himself, including Fidel Castro in 1994, Michael Jordan and Arnold Schwarzenegger. Cigar Aficionado today has an audience of more than 1.3 million people. Shanken also founded the trade publications Market Watch and Shanken News Daily, and was honoured with the Association of Magazine Media's Lifetime Achievement Award in 2019.
