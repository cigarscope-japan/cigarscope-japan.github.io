5th Avenue's Insolvency Leaves Habanos Distribution in Three European Markets in Limbo

The Habanos distributor for Germany, Austria and Poland entered formal insolvency on Nov. 1, weeks after EU sanctions left it unable to fulfil orders, Halfwheel reports.

Published
November 8, 2026
Announced
November 6, 2026
Maker
Habanos S.A.

The distributor that supplies Cuban cigars to three European markets is now formally insolvent. 5th Avenue Products Trading GmbH, the exclusive Habanos distributor for Germany, Austria and Poland, entered formal insolvency proceedings in a German court on Nov. 1, Halfwheel reported on Nov. 3.

According to the insolvency administrator, the company has both funds and inventory, but is effectively unable to operate because of EU sanctions — described as the sole cause of the insolvency. The company itself is not suspected of wrongdoing. 5th Avenue announced in September that it could no longer fulfil orders, after EU sanctions introduced in late July froze distribution across its three markets — sanctions aimed at Chen Zhi, the businessman accused in the United States whose Allied Cigar Corporation owns 50% of Habanos S.A.

What is at stake

The administrator has pointed to the company's 50 employees, the sales lost by tobacconists across Germany and the tax revenue at stake, and says the search for a solution is urgent. Germany is one of the top five markets for Habanos, according to Habanos S.A., and the halt comes on top of existing shortages and price rises for Cuban cigars.

On Nov. 6 came a further development: a British Virgin Islands court ruled that winding-up orders over more than 30 Chen-linked entities should be lifted (see the related story). That may calm the ownership dispute, but the U.S. proceedings and EU sanctions continue separately — so, as Tobacco Reporter puts the wider picture, a restart of distribution is likely to take time.

Editorial note

The insolvency account is based on Halfwheel's reporting of Nov. 3, 2026, and the ownership context on Tobacco Reporter. Any restart of distribution would also depend on sanctions and court outcomes that remain unresolved.
