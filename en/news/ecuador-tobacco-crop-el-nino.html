Super El Niño Destroys More Than Half of A.S.P.'s 2026 Ecuador Crop

A.S.P. Enterprises has told clients to expect only 25–35 percent of their orders after excessive rain destroyed more than half of its 2026 crop — a blow to the wrapper supply behind a third of the new cigars launched at PCA 2026.

Published
November 9, 2026
Announced
November 4, 2026
Maker
A.S.P. Enterprises

A.S.P. Enterprises, one of Ecuador's largest tobacco growers, has informed clients who buy its Ecuadorian tobacco that they should expect only 25 to 35 percent of what they ordered, after this year's Super El Niño brought excessive rain that destroyed a significant share of the company's 2026 crop, Halfwheel reported. Company president and CEO David Perez said 40 percent of the crop was still in the field and at risk unless the rainfall reversed: of the 18-week growing season, the first six weeks went well, the middle six had problems, and the last six, he said, have been a disaster.

For wrapper leaf, rain is especially damaging. Wrapper must keep a flawless appearance, and excessive rain can bring mold or spotting while the accompanying wind physically tears leaves, rendering them unusable. Ecuador grows no cigars — it has no cigar factories — but it is one of the most important countries in the trade: the vast majority of Connecticut shade cigars wear Ecuadorian wrapper, and a Halfwheel analysis of the cigars introduced at the 2026 PCA trade show found that a third of them used a wrapper from Ecuador.

Because harvested leaf must be aged and processed before it is rolled, consumers will not feel the effects for a few years — factories will first see the reduced supply next year. But Perez warned that the industry will feel this Super El Niño more than other bad crops: yields have collapsed at the same time as many factories, facing rising prices, have reduced the wrapper stocks they keep on hand.
