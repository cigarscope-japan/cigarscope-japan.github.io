White House Pulls FDA Commissioner Nominee Ahead of Senate Vote

The White House pulled the nomination of Dr. Heidi Overton as FDA commissioner on the night of November 17, just before a Senate health committee vote scheduled for November 18. According to Halfwheel, the White House pulled the nomination of Dr. Heidi Overton as commissioner of the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) on the night of November 17, just before a Senate health committee vote scheduled for November 18. The FDA is the U.S. federal agency that regulates tobacco, so who leads it matters for the future of cigar regulation.

Administration officials explained that the votes needed for Senate confirmation were not there. Sen. Bill Cassidy, the committee's Republican chair, countered that the votes existed both in committee and on the floor, and said the withdrawal came because the administration refused assurances sought by opponents. No successor candidate has been announced. The name of Sen. Roger Marshall of Kansas has been mentioned, but there are also reports that the administration is not considering him, and the situation remains fluid.

The FDA has been without a permanent commissioner since Marty Makary stepped down in May, Halfwheel reported. Acting commissioner Kyle Diamantis leads the agency, but an acting term is capped at 210 days; the withdrawal reset that clock, so the acting arrangement will continue for the time being. A permanent commissioner is not expected to be decided until after January, when Democrats take the Senate majority.
