Cuba's 2025-26 Harvest: 11,426 Hectares in the Vuelta Abajo, Up on Last Year but Short of Target

The 2025-26 season ended with 11,426 hectares planted in the Vuelta Abajo, up from about 10,500 the year before but well short of the 14,000-hectare target, as rain, drought and the energy crisis cut into the crop.

Published
November 9, 2026
Announced
March 17, 2026
Maker
Habanos S.A.

The 2025–26 tobacco season in Cuba’s Vuelta Abajo has ended with 11,426 hectares planted, according to Cuban press reports citing officials of the state company Tabacuba, carried by Cigars-connect. The figure is a slight increase on the previous season — Pinar del Río, the province that is home to the Vuelta Abajo, harvested approximately 10,500 hectares in 2024–25 — but it falls well short of the initial target of over 14,000 hectares.

Weather and energy both worked against the crop. Heavy rains in September destroyed 19,000 seed plants, drought disrupted the planting schedule in the north of the province, and the country’s energy crisis struck directly at production: 50 percent of the province’s tobacco fields are irrigated using electrified systems.

Tabacuba’s answer is to change the energy mix. During the previous season 213 photovoltaic irrigation systems were installed, work is under way on another 832, and 2,000 more are on order to arrive in August, so that at least 50 percent of irrigation will be covered by solar energy for the 2026–27 season. Pinar del Río typically accounts for 60 to 70 percent of the tobacco harvest destined for Cuba’s cigar exports.
